Overview

One product powering two consumer energy brands

Zelora and Enreal are consumer-facing apps that give solar and battery owners visibility over their home energy — generation, consumption, export, and savings. Both products run on the same underlying platform, designed at Evergen. My role covered the full UX and UI for both apps, along with the white-label design system that allows a single product to serve two distinct brands without rebuilding from scratch.

Zelora is a Bunnings-backed product for homeowners with rooftop solar and battery storage. Enreal serves home electrification customers — households transitioning away from gas to an all-electric home. The design brief was consistent across both: make complex, real-time energy data legible and motivating for everyday homeowners, not energy professionals.

Key Decisions

Four decisions that shaped the product

Anchoring the home screen in a house

Early design sprint explorations used spider diagrams and radial data visualisations — technically accurate representations of energy flow, but ones that required interpretation to read. The decision was made to anchor the home screen around a house graphic: a central metaphor that homeowners orient to immediately, with energy flows mapped to the familiar parts of their home rather than to abstract chart segments.

Designing for two levels of engagement

Research pointed to a clear split in how users engage with their energy data: most want a quick status check — is my solar generating, am I exporting to the grid, am I saving money? A smaller but active group wants to go much deeper into real-time flows and historical data. Rather than designing two separate interfaces or landing on one middle ground that served neither well, a toggle was introduced directly on the home screen. Users can switch between a simplified summary view and a detailed live chart view in one tap — the simple view is the default, the chart view is always accessible.

Making savings legible, not just visible

Showing a kilowatt-hour figure alongside a dollar amount is accurate but rarely meaningful to a homeowner glancing at their phone. The savings screen was designed to break returns down by source — solar generation, battery discharge, and tariff optimisation — and includes an estimated bill savings figure that maps directly to what users see on their electricity bill. Carbon savings are expressed in relatable equivalents so users can connect their system's output to something tangible.

White-label as a first-class design constraint

Zelora and Enreal have distinct brand identities — Zelora's palette is warm and Bunnings-aligned; Enreal's is clean and teal. Designing for both from the outset meant treating brand as a variable from the beginning of the component system, not something applied after the fact. The white-label architecture separates brand tokens — colour, typography, logo — from layout and interaction logic, so a brand swap produces a coherent, branded experience across every screen without touching the underlying structure. The same approach extended to feature architecture: components and flows were built to be added to, so the team could continue shipping without reworking the foundation.

Web Portal

A desktop companion built for longer sessions

The web portal was designed for the same users as the app, but in a desktop context where more screen real estate and a longer session changes how energy data should be presented. The dashboard surfaces historical charts, seven-day energy views, and detailed breakdowns — content that exists on mobile but is significantly more useful when a user can see it at full width across multiple panels simultaneously.

The telemetry charts in the portal are intentionally more detailed than the mobile equivalents. Desktop users who choose to open the portal are signalling a different intent to someone doing a quick home screen check — the interface reflects that. The portal launched alongside the mobile app, giving users access to both from day one.

Outcome

Launched across two brands, with a foundation built to grow

Zelora and Enreal launched on iOS with the web portal available at the same time. The underlying design system — components, tokens, layout logic — was structured to be extended, and the team continued shipping new features on top of the same foundations after the initial release. The white-label architecture held up across both brands without diverging into separate codebases or design files.
